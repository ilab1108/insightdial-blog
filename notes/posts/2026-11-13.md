## LinkedIn post

Not every duplicate call is fraud. But every unclear duplicate rule becomes a dispute.

A caller gets cut off and rings back. Someone calls twice to compare quotes. The same offer runs on two sites. All of these produce repeat calls, and if nobody agreed in advance how to treat them, the buyer and the seller will count them differently.

Three things fix most of it:

- Write the rule down. Agree the window (24 hours? 30 days?) and the scope (per buyer, per campaign or network-wide) in every buyer agreement.
- Enforce it before billing. Repeat-caller blocks keep duplicates away from buyers who will not pay for them, and re-routing sends those callers to a buyer whose rules allow them.
- Watch it per source. A traffic source sending far more repeats or very short calls than the rest needs attention long before month end.

Also check why callers ring back. Calls landing on a closed office or a capped buyer create many of the repeats in the first place.

## Community reply

Before arguing over duplicate calls, agree with the buyer on the window (for example 24 hours or 30 days) and whether it applies per buyer or across the whole network. Many repeats are just dropped calls ringing back, so checking buyer hours and caps before the transfer helps too.
